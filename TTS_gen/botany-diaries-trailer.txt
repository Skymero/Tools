There was a time when scientists studied the world.

Then came a time when they had to rebuild it.

After war left enormous regions of the planet barren, humanity gathered inside cities designed to survive where the old world could not. Solar membranes harvested the desert sun. Greenhouses climbed through towers. Engineers rebuilt water systems, botanists designed plants for poisoned soil, and every successful experiment pushed civilization a little farther beyond the safety of its walls.

In this world, scientists became heroes.

Kellie never wanted to be one.

She was a botanist working in one of Solar City’s experimental nurseries, spending fourteen-hour days keeping other people’s research alive while quietly planning the life she actually wanted.

A little plant shop.

A few shelves.

A propagation bench.

Maybe enough money to stop working for Bram Vale.

She kept telling herself she only needed one more year of experience before she was ready.

Then one night, an experimental plant began releasing something into the air.

And when Kellie looked at it…

the plant looked back.

At first, she blamed exhaustion.

Then the plants started talking.

They wore her boss’s face. They mocked her. They knew things she had never said aloud.

And soon Kellie faced a problem more frightening than any failed experiment:

She could no longer trust what she saw.

But cameras could.

Sensors could.

Measurements could.

So Kellie did what scientists have always done when human perception fails.

She tested reality.

And that was when she discovered something worse.

Some of the impossible things she was seeing weren't hallucinations.

Plants really were moving.

People throughout the city were changing.

And whatever had escaped that greenhouse wasn't finished with humanity.

Kellie wanted a quiet life surrounded by plants.

Instead, she may become the person who discovers what plants—and people—are becoming.

This is **Botany Diaries**.

A story about science, survival, trauma, and a botanist who became the hero no one expected…

while trying very hard to prove that the plants were not actually talking to her.
